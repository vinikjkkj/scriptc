THE FLOOR THAT APPLIES TO EVERY "YES" THIS SLICE CAN PRODUCE
  At 3 v 3 there are C(6,3) = 20 arm assignments, so the smallest attainable
  two-sided permutation p is 2/20 = 0.10. NO effect, however large, reaches
  p < 0.05 at this n without leaning on normality. Every YES reported is
  parametric and will say so. The Welch df collapses toward 2 with unequal
  variances and the two-sided 95% bar there is 4.303, not the 2.776 of df=4;
  the df used is printed per row.

WHAT IS READ FIRST, BEFORE ANY COMPARISON
  HCPKCOVER = snapPrivate / kernelPeakPagefile. A snapshot with low coverage
  is a reading of a trough near the peak, not of the peak. vmcensus's
  committed standard is 99.85-99.96%. The two committed peak runs that exist
  (evidence-peak abba1A, abba4A, both STACKLESS, step 512 KB) read 99.547%
  and 99.579% -- already BELOW that standard. Coverage for all six runs is
  reported before any delta. A run outside the stated bar does not enter the
  statistics and is named with its reason.

  >>> THE HALF MOST LIKELY TO BE WRONG IS P3, AND IT CONTAMINATES P1 AND P2.
  The snapshot is taken by a POLLING thread (5 ms interval, 1 MiB step by
  default). If coverage varies between runs -- and worse, if it varies
  DIFFERENTLY between lanes, because the two programs climb at different
  rates -- then the arms are not the same instant and the comparison is not
  peak against peak. In that case the result of this slice is "the instrument
  does not give two comparable arms at this n", and that is a legitimate
  result I want to have. The two committed runs already read 99.55/99.58
  rather than 99.9, which is weak prior evidence AGAINST P3 as worded.
  A SECOND NAMED RISK: position bimodality, handled by alternating and by the
  promised leave-one-out, and no conclusion will be drawn from run 1 vs run 2.
